The Korean Prince Locked in a Rice Chest by His Own Father | K-Mystery

## Description
In the summer of 1762, in the royal palace of Joseon Korea, a prince was dragged into a small wooden rice chest. The lid was shut. The lock was turned. And the man who ordered it... was his own father, the king.

For eight days, Crown Prince Sado remained inside. No food. No water. No escape. On the eighth day, he died.

This is not a legend. It is one of the most documented royal executions in Asian history, recorded in the official chronicles of the Joseon Dynasty. And the question that still haunts historians today is simple: Was Crown Prince Sado truly insane... or was he the victim of a political conspiracy?

In this video, we explore:
- The turbulent childhood of Crown Prince Sado under King Yeongjo's harsh rule
- Signs of psychological distress and the debate over mental illness vs. political fabrication
- The role of Lady Hyegyeong's memoir and its political motivations
- The execution method that avoided spilling royal blood
- How Sado's son, King Jeongjo, spent his reign rehabilitating his father's memory

The Joseon Dynasty (1392-1897) was one of the longest-ruling dynasties in world history, and its royal court was a place of breathtaking beauty and unimaginable cruelty. The story of Prince Sado is one of its darkest chapters.

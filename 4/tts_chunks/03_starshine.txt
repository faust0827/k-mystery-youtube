Here is where the story stops being a simple ban.

The palace had its own shamans. They were called gungmu, state shamans, and they were housed in an office inside the palace grounds: the Seongsucheong. The Star Shrine.

Their job was to pray for the royal family and to avert disaster. This was not a secret. It was an institution, with a budget and a staff.

And when the country needed rain, the state called the shamans.

In 1410, King Taejong gathered seventy shamans at Baegaksan to pray for rain. In 1436, King Sejong gave shamans rice to perform rain rites, and the record notes that the rain came. Again and again, the Annals show the same pattern. The Confucian state condemning shamanic ritual in public, and requesting it in private.

The Star Shrine survived for over a century. In 1503, King Yeonsangun, no friend of Confucian officials, stated plainly that the office of state shamans was ancient, and should continue.

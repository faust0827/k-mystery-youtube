In 1477, outside the gates of the Joseon capital, a group of shamans performed a ritual.

Someone reported it. The court asked who had ordered it.

The answer: the palace had. The king's own wet nurse and a royal eunuch had gone there to pray.

The king's response was a single line, recorded in the Annals: "Do not do this again."

That is the whole story of Joseon in one exchange. For five hundred years, this Confucian state outlawed shamanism, taxed it, exiled its practitioners, and wrote laws against it. And for five hundred years, the palace kept calling them back.

In 1478, King Seongjong lost patience.

The shamans had been expelled from the capital, and then they had simply come back. The prohibition had loosened. They were practicing in the city again, openly, without the slightest hesitation.

His order to the Censorate is one of the most revealing lines in the Annals. "It has become the custom. The lewd rites flourish. The ignorant crowd flocks to them. It cannot be left unprohibited."

Read that again. It is the sound of a state admitting that its law is not working.

So the laws got harsher. Shamans caught inside the capital were punished. Officials who failed to arrest them were dismissed. And the state began to tax them, the muse, partly to squeeze them out of business, partly because it needed the money. In 1464, the government ordered a register of every shaman, compiled every three years, so the tax could be collected properly. Later records list the rates. The chief state shaman paid nine geun. An ordinary shaman paid one.

And yet. In 1684, a shaman named Makrye was caught inside the palace, performing rituals, and wearing the king's own dragon robe. She was sentenced to exile on an island.

In 1743, King Yeongjo signed a law ordering any shaman who entered the palace in royal robes to be banished to a remote island.

By then, the pattern was unmistakable. Every generation passed a new law against the same practice. You do not need a new law for something that has stopped happening.
